moon_temp_tracer — Centralized Dual-Thermistor Bridge (Rev E)

Both thermistors are now the two active arms of one bridge — the ADC reads the difference between the moonlit and shaded sensors directly in hardware, plus each sensor's absolute reading on the side. One ADS1115, one LM4040 (down from two of each in Rev D). Sensors are ~12" apart on the tracking rig; lead resistance at that length is negligible.

Even at worst-case, PGA ±0.512V covers a >20°C differential without clipping — generous headroom for a presumably subtle effect. ~780-2900 counts/°C depending on baseline — 2-9× better than Rev D.

Critical notes

  • Direction is unambiguous everywhere in range: NTC resistance and bridge midpoint voltage both move monotonically, so sign(V_A − V_B) reliably tells you which sensor is colder — no sign reversal anywhere from -30°C to 45°C.
  • Rf_A and Rf_B tempco now matters more than in a symmetric bridge: the two resistors sit at genuinely different temperatures from each other (that's the point), so their ±5ppm/°C spec directly sets the false-offset floor, not just long-term drift.
  • POT1 nulls a constant mismatch (resistor tolerance, lead asymmetry, ADC offset) at one calibration temperature. It does not correct for the two thermistors' B-value tolerance (±0.3%) diverging at other temperatures — that residual still needs empirical calibration, same as the existing Moon_Temp_Calibration_Jupyter workflow.
  • Both absolute channels (AIN0, AIN2) let you cross-check the differential signal against two independent readings — catches a case where the bridge shows an apparent bias that isn't corroborated by the absolute temperatures.
